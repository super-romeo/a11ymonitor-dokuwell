How it works

Accessibility problems rarely come from one big mistake. They creep in with every new page, plugin and redesign: an image uploaded without alternative text, a button that only has an icon, a form field without a label, grey text that is hard to read on a white background.

Fixing them once is not enough. A site that was checked last year has changed many times since, and nobody knows which of those changes added new problems. Manual audits are thorough but occasional, and between them the site drifts.

The service scans your pages on a schedule and keeps watching. You add your site, choose which pages or sections to include, and set how often scans run: weekly, daily, or after each release.

Each scan runs automated checks based on the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG). It looks for issues that software can detect reliably: missing alternative text, low colour contrast, empty links and buttons, missing form labels, headings out of order, missing page language and similar problems.

Results are shown per page and per issue type. For each issue you see where it is on the page, which element causes it and a short explanation of why it matters and how it is usually fixed. Issues that repeat across many pages are grouped, so a single template fix is visible as one task.

When a scan finds issues that a page did not have before, you get an alert by email or in a team chat with the list of affected pages. Problems introduced by a release are noticed in days, not at the next audit.

A history is kept for every site: how many issues were found in each scan, how many were fixed and how many are new. Agencies can share a short report with each client, and in-house teams can see whether the site is getting better.

Nothing needs to be installed on your site; scans run from outside, the way a visitor's browser loads the page. Pages behind a login can be added with a test account.

Automated checks find many issues but not all of them. Whether a page makes sense with a screen reader or can be used with a keyboard alone still needs a person to test it. The service shows what can be checked automatically and leaves the rest to your manual review.

The service is built for site owners, agencies and web teams anywhere. It is in preparation: we are checking whether site owners need it before it is built.

The service finds and tracks issues; fixing them stays with the site owner or developer, and a scan alone does not make a site fully accessible.

Questions

What does a scan check?

Automated checks based on WCAG: alternative text, contrast, links and buttons, form labels, headings and more.

Do I need to install anything on my site?

No, pages are scanned from outside, the way a browser loads them.

How often are pages scanned?

Weekly, daily or after each release — you choose.

Does it make my site fully accessible?

No. It finds and tracks issues that software can detect; some checks need a person, and fixes stay with you.
